The quality of the generated prompt is capped by the quality of your brief. The brief scorer flags issues — this guide shows what good looks like. Three before/after examples across the most-used modes.
🎬YouTube Strategist
Weak brief
"I want to make a video about productivity for my channel."
Result: Generic tips-and-habits script. Indistinguishable from 10,000 other videos. AI has nothing to work with.
Strong brief
"Script for my 52k subscriber channel (busy professionals, 25–40) on why most productivity systems fail within 2 weeks. Unique angle: behavioural science + my personal story of failing every system I tried. 10–12 min. High retention. Hook must land within 30 seconds."
Result: Specific payoffs, tension arc, evidence-backed claims, differentiated hook. The AI has an angle to defend.
Added: audience defined · length specified · unique angle stated · hook constraint · personal differentiation
⚙️Code Architect
Weak brief
"Build me a todo app."
Result: Generic React todo with local state. No error handling. No mention of your actual constraints. The AI guessed everything.
Strong brief
"React 18 + TypeScript todo app for enterprise users who need audit trails. Must work offline (PWA). Constraints: no external state libraries — useReducer only. Must handle 10k+ items without lag. Done = CRUD + local persistence + CSV export + zero TypeScript errors."
Result: Architecture that addresses audit trails, offline mode, performance, and the no-library constraint before a single line is written.
Added: tech stack · user context · explicit constraints · performance requirement · clear definition of done
📊Business Strategist
Weak brief
"I want to start a SaaS business."
Result: Generic advice about market research and MVPs. No pressure on your specific assumptions. Useless.
Strong brief
"Validating a SaaS for solo law practitioners (50k in the US). Competing with Clio and MyCase. Differentiator: AI-assisted document templates. Budget: $50k runway. Key question: is the differentiator defensible, or will Clio ship it in 6 months?"
Result: Assumption audit focused on the specific market, named competitors, and the one question that determines whether to proceed.
Added: specific market · competitor names · differentiator stated · budget context · exact question to answer
The AI selects from these and builds a PRIMARY / SECONDARY / VERIFY stack based on your brief. It must declare its choices, execution order, and ruled-out conflicts in its first reply — and end every subsequent response with a STACK AUDIT line.

★
Stack audit block — every AI response must end with AUDIT: STACK INTACT or AUDIT: DRIFT — [description]. Closes the declaration-theatre gap where the AI declares a stack, then ignores it.
★
Multi-mode stacking — select up to 2 modes. 8 pre-built popular pairs (Technical Co-Founder, Go-to-Market, etc.). Dual prompts run Phase 1 → handoff → Phase 2 with one unified technique stack across both phases.

Notes field on every history entry — inline editable, auto-saves on blur. History is now a session log, not just a list of inputs.
★
Brief scorer rewritten — now penalises vague language ("something", "interesting", "good") and requires minimum word count before showing any green signals. Measures information density, not just keyword presence.
★
"Strong Briefs" guide replaces changelog as default help — 3 before/after examples showing exactly what moves the quality needle. Changelog moved to its own tab.
